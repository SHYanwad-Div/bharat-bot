The problem

US sidewalk robots work because US sidewalks are smooth

Robots like Starship's have made more than 10 million deliveries on pavements like this. None of the makers publishes how deep a pothole its robot can cross or how much water it can wade through, and an Indian street asks for both, plus a plan for dogs, cows and auto-rickshaws.

Three tests

Drop a US-style robot onto an Indian street

The standard robot is modelled on today's sidewalk robots: 9 cm wheels that climb 6 cm, no way to judge water, a locked lid with no alarm, and no plan for dogs or traffic. Bharat-Bot drives the same street.

The India-adapted design

Meet Bharat-Bot

  • 32 cm wheels on a rocker-bogieKeeps all six wheels down and climbs about 18 cm.
  • Depth camera + LiDARSees holes and standing water, not just obstacles.
  • Freeze protocol for street dogsStop, go quiet, let them lose interest. No ultrasonics.
  • OTP lid + tamper alarmLift or tilt it: siren, photo and live GPS to the ops centre.
  • Traffic-aware plannerKeeps left and yields before it steps into the lane.
  • Sealed for the monsoonIP67 motors, wades up to 8 cm, refuses deeper water.

Biggest caveat. The dog result rests on one hypothesis from the film's model: a still, silent robot makes a pack lose interest about five times faster than one that has merely stopped. If any still robot calmed dogs that well, the upgraded standard robot would get past the pack on 21% of streets instead of 0%. A siren on the lid is enough to stop a thief; dogs and broken, flooded roads are where Bharat-Bot's design wins. Full write-up, with seeds you can replay: research/evaluation.md.

Safety architecture

AI suggests. Safety decides. Hardware protects.

The same rule as GH-01, Sumith Shridhar's greenhouse digital twin. The AI may propose anything; a safety controller with hard limits approves or vetoes it; and hard-wired cut-offs still protect people and the robot if every computer fails.

1

AI suggests

  • Detects dogs, cows, potholes, water, people and autos
  • Proposes a path and a speed
2

Safety controller decides

  • Dog within 6 m → stop
  • Unknown water → no-go
  • Person within 1.2 m → hold
  • Link lost for more than 2 s → hold
3

Hard-wired cut-offs protect

  • Bumper switches and an e-stop
  • Tilt over 25° cuts the drive
  • Battery over-temperature cut-off
  • Mechanical lid lock

Every decision in the simulation is logged as SENSE → AI → SAFETY → ACT, and the film's control-room HUD replays that log frame by frame.

Where it can win

Only when it's busy. Gated societies, campuses, hospitals, night and monsoon hours. Not open streets, where a courier is cheap.

Honest limits

What we don't know yet

  • Everything here is simulated. The street ranges come from the research, but no value was measured on a real Indian street. The physics is kinematic (no wheel slip, tipping, battery or sensor noise), and perception is perfect for the robots that have it.
  • No verified open-street sidewalk-robot pilot exists in India yet.
  • The dog protocol is untested with a real robot. It follows expert advice (don't run; stop and let the dog lose interest), and ultrasonic repellers show no proven effect, so Bharat-Bot doesn't use them.
  • No robot maker publishes a wading depth. Knee-deep floods are a no-go: Bharat-Bot stops and calls an operator.
  • Big packs and dense crowds: hold and call a remote operator.
  • Costs are paper estimates.

Where Bharat-Bot still fails in the 200 runs

  • On 13 of 200 full deliveries, water deeper than its 8 cm limit covered every way through, so it stopped 1 m short and called an operator. That is the intended safe refusal.
  • Once in 200 it drove into a person who stepped inside its stopping distance: it stops for people in its path but doesn't predict where they walk.
  • Its wheels still clip potholes on most runs (2.4 per 100 m, against 3.9 for the blind standard robot). The suspension, not the planner, makes that harmless.
  • Its pothole swerves don't check for traffic behind, so it made the auto-rickshaw brake or pass close on 6% of passes.

How it's built

Python → Blender → Remotion

01 · sim/

Simulate in Python

A lattice path planner, rocker-bogie and rigid-chassis terrain kinematics, a dog pack that alerts, chases and loses interest, a thief, a cow, an auto-rickshaw and the customer. The runs are deterministic, so the film and its HUD come from the same run.

02 · Blender 5.2

Build and render

sim/build_scene.py turns a simulation run into an animated shot: procedural robots, people, auto-rickshaw and street, Poly Haven textures, Quaternius dogs and cow, rendered with EEVEE.

03 · video/

Cut the film in Remotion

React composes the film: the control-room HUD is drawn from per-frame telemetry, the results from evaluation.json, and the tanpura-drone music is synthesised in Python.

# the four simulations, with their event logs (about 25 s)
python sim/simulate.py all
# 200 random streets per test and robot, writes research/evaluation.md (about 5 min)
python sim/evaluate.py
# one seeded street, any robot
python sim/simulate.py bharat_full --seed 7 --robot baseline
